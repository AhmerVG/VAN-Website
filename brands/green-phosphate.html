The problem

Pakistan imports phosphate. Then the soil takes most of it back.

80–95% of Pakistani soils are phosphorus-deficient, and soil pH sits far above the 6.0–7.0 window where phosphorus stays available. On calcareous ground, free calcium captures most of what a farmer spreads before a root ever reaches it.

Phosphorus is not lost to the air like nitrogen, or leached like potassium. It is still there — but in the season the farmer paid for it, the crop recovers only about a fifth. It is the largest single line in Pakistan’s fertilizer import bill, bought in dollars and locked up in the first season.

Phosphorus-use efficiency

The label counts kilograms in the bag. The crop counts kilograms it can take.

The technology

Three things happen around this granule that do not happen around DAP.

What is engineered in, beyond the two declared nutrients.

1 · ACID

Acid at the granule surface

The granule carries 3% free phosphoric acid — a measured COA figure — while its bulk reaction stays near neutral at pH (1%) 6.5–7.5. The phosphate dissolves into a micro-environment that is not the alkaline reaction zone DAP creates, so it stays in solution long enough for a root to find it.

2 · HUMIC

A humic coating

Humic substances are coated onto the granule. They chelate the calcium, iron and aluminium that would otherwise capture the phosphate, and they improve soil structure and biology where they land.

3 · SUGARS

Fermentable sugars

Bio-available fermentable sugars feed the soil microbial population, raising phosphatase activity — the enzyme route by which stable organic phosphorus is mineralised back into an available form.

4 · RESTRAINT

Restrained nitrogen

Nitrogen is held at a basal 6% rather than loaded in. High ammoniacal nitrogen at sowing risks ammonia injury to a germinating seedling; this grade is built to sit safely beside seed.

Magnesium and a biostimulant fraction are also part of the formulation. They are named without figures because they are not label-declared.

Against the default

Green Phosphate and DAP are not the same trade.

DAP is the phosphate most Pakistani growers buy. This is what differs.

Di-ammonium phosphate

A true salt, high in ammoniacal nitrogen, alkaline in its immediate reaction zone.
  • Excess ammoniacal nitrogen at the basal stage — risk of seedling ammonia injury
  • Creates an alkaline micro-zone — the condition that fixes phosphate
  • No humic acid, no biostimulant, no organic-matter contribution
  • Phosphorus efficiency low on calcareous ground

Green Phosphate

An acidic, humic-coated granule carrying only as much nitrogen as a basal dose needs.
  • Controlled nitrogen at 6% — safe next to germinating seed
  • No alkaline reaction zone — phosphate stays available where the root is
  • Humic acid and fermentable sugars raise soil biology and phosphatase activity
  • Phosphorus protected from fixation for longer

VAN publishes no head-to-head efficiency percentage between the two. The chemistry is the argument.

Right place

Phosphorus does not travel. Placement is most of the agronomy.

Three ways in, in order of preference — the root has to reach the granule, because the granule will not reach the root.

1 · BEST

With the seed drill

Places the granule in the seed row at sowing, exactly where the first roots will emerge, in one pass.

2

Band placement

A concentrated band beside or below the row — saturates the fixing capacity of a small soil volume rather than feeding all of it.

3 · FALLBACK

Broadcasting

Even spread at land preparation, worked in. Simplest and most common — and the one that exposes the most granule surface to the most soil.
