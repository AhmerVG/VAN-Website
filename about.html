It starts on that research farm and ends at the gate: what a trial shows sets the formulation, what the formulation becomes is made or grown on the same site, tested in the laboratory before it ships, and registered against a Pakistan Standard before it is sold. Most of what leaves carries somebody else’s name. VAN is primarily a business-to-business manufacturer, for 14 national and multinational clients, and Vital Green, founded in 2018, carries the rest direct to more than 10,000 farmers.

Origin

Crop nutrition was sold by the bag, not by what the crop needs at each stage.

Before VAN existed, nutrition was sold as 1 bag of this, 3 bags of that. The same prescription for a cotton grower on light soil and a rice grower on a heavy alkaline field in the next district. VAN’s founders saw that in the fields, season after season.

In 2007, VAN’s founder produced humic acid in Pakistan from Pakistani leonardite, the oxidised lignite humic substances are drawn from, a material until then imported and, as far as VAN knows, not made here before. What followed built the rest: more chemistries were tried, then tested on ground VAN came to own. That research farm is what the company was built on.

Evidence

More went on. Less came back.

Between 1999-2000 and 2023-24 Pakistan raised the nutrient it applies per acre by 68%. Wheat yield rose by 31%. Every kilogram now returns less than it did at the turn of the century, which is an argument about balance and timing, not about applying more.

Between 1999-2000 and 2023-24 the nutrient Pakistan applied per acre rose by 68%, from 38.6 to 64.9 kg (arable land, World Bank, published as 95.4 and 160.3 kg/ha). Wheat yield rose by 31%. Each kilogram of nutrient now returns 22% less wheat than it did in 1999-2000.

The argument VAN was built around is set out, with every source under it, on the national picture page.

The nutrition Pakistan pays for →

Yield per kilogram of nutrient is a yield index divided by a nutrient-per-acre index, 2000 = 100. It sets an all-crop nutrient denominator against a crop-specific yield, so it assumes the split of nutrient between crops has not shifted dramatically. It is a partial factor productivity indicator, not a nutrient balance. The direction is robust; the exact magnitude is not, and we would not defend it to the decimal.
