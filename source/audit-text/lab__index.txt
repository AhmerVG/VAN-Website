Why
Why VAN built a laboratory
VAN Lab is built to return the same answer twice on the same sample. Written methods, calibrated instruments, reference standards run alongside every batch, two analysts on every sample, a manager who reviews the work and signs his name to it.
All of that exists so that the number does not depend on who ran the test, on which day they ran it, or on what anybody hoped it would say.
8 of the 21 tests VAN Lab performs are covered by PNAC ISO/IEC 17025:2017 accreditation, LAB 336. The other 13 are tests the laboratory runs which sit outside that granted scope. They are listed separately throughout this site so you always know which is which.

01
The blind chain
Nobody who tested your sample knew it was yours.
The laboratory is arranged so that this holds. Your sample is coded before it reaches a bench, split three ways, and run by two analysts working independently.
Step
What happens
Your sample arrives, not at the laboratory
Somebody has to open the parcel; no arrangement removes that, and a laboratory claiming otherwise is describing something that cannot happen. So the parcel is not opened at the laboratory. Sample Reception is a separate place with its own address, independent of the laboratory both physically and organisationally. The people there know who you are. They will never see your result.
Your name comes off
Reception enters your details and the system generates a laboratory code. The system, not a person, because an assigned code can carry meaning and meaning leaks. The code encodes nothing about you: no initials, no district, no segment, no priority marker. Your form, the outer packaging, the courier documents and every brand marking stay at the desk.
Split three ways
Two portions go forward for analysis. One is retained, untouched, so a complete independent re-test is possible if you ever dispute the result. All three carry the code and nothing else.
Two analysts, working independently
VAN does not report on a single analysis. Two different analysts each receive a portion and each runs the test, and neither sees the other’s result before both are submitted. If the two compared results first, their agreement would prove nothing.
The two results are compared
Against a documented acceptance criterion. Where they agree, the result is reported. Where they do not, the QC Manager decides whether a third analysis is run or which result stands.
Reviewed and signed
The Assistant QC Manager reviews the work; the QC Manager signs the report digitally. He signs against a code and does not know whose sample he is approving, which is precisely what makes the signature worth having.
The report finds you again
Only at release does the code reconnect to you, and the system does it, not a person. The report reaches you and nobody in the laboratory learns who it went to.

Book and track
No account, no login.
What to know before booking
What to send
500 g by weight, whether the product is solid or liquid. Enough for two independent analyses plus the portion we retain in case you ever dispute the result. Liquids in a sealed bottle. Bring it to the laboratory, hand it to your VAN dealer, or courier it.
Turnaround
The clock starts when your booking is confirmed, not when the sample reaches the bench. Most tests are 3 working days; humic acid is 4, because the determination alone runs 36 hours.
What you get back
The measured value for each parameter you asked for, and the method used to measure it. A measured value only means something alongside the method that produced it. Particularly for humic acid, where the extraction protocol defines the number.
Already have a VAN bag?
Send us the batch number off the bag instead and we will give you the certificate of analysis for that batch. No charge, no sample needed.
Anyone with a reference number can ask where a sample is, no account, and you do not have to be a regular customer. The tracker on this page is built and is not yet reading from the laboratory system, so for now send the reference on WhatsApp and somebody looks it up the same day.
04
For distributors, importers and other labs
Three different worries, one protocol.
Distributors. Incoming goods
You are about to sell stock on your own name. If it is not what your supplier says it is, the farmer comes back to you, not to them. We test anything you stock, not only VAN product. A laboratory that tested only its own company’s range would be no use to you for incoming goods.
Importers. Consignment testing
A consignment sitting at a port costs money every day it sits. What you need is a turnaround you can plan around and a certificate your buyer will accept: 3 working days for most tests from confirmed booking, printed on the price list rather than quoted case by case, and PNAC ISO/IEC 17025:2017 LAB 336 for the accredited parameters. Non-accredited tests are identified as such on the price list and on the report.
Other laboratories. Reference and umpire analysis
When two parties disagree about a result, somebody has to be the third. That role only works if the third laboratory has no stake in the answer, and cannot acquire one. A referee sample entering VAN Lab is stripped of its origin at the same desk as a farmer’s bag. The analysts do not know which laboratory sent it, which two parties are in dispute, or what either of them reported. They receive a code and a test request.
Testing more than the occasional sample? Distributors and importers running regular volume are quoted differently. We are working out a block-testing arrangement and would rather build it around real submission volumes than publish a number we have not thought through. Tell us roughly how many samples a season and we will talk.
