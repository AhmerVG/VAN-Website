How the Punjab soil samples shaped our formulations

The Punjab soil testing programme tested 770,160 soil samples from 36 districts of Punjab in about 2016 to 2018. VAN did not run this programme. We read every row of the programme's 36 district workbooks. We base every formulation on these samples and on our 17 years of trial results. Of these samples, 69.1% had a pH above 8. Soil with a pH above 8 is alkaline. We therefore write each of our crop plans for alkaline, calcareous soil. Calcareous soil is rich in calcium carbonate, also called lime.

69.1%

pH above 8

In the programme, 69.1% of the samples had a pH above 8. In soil above pH 8, part of the nitrogen from urea left on the soil surface is lost to the air as ammonia.

Our product for this soil condition is Vital Urea.

97.9%

Phosphorus below 15 ppm

In the programme, 97.9% of the samples had less than 15 parts per million (ppm) of available phosphorus. A soil needs at least 15 ppm to be counted as adequate in phosphorus. In calcareous soil, free calcium binds most of the phosphate applied as fertiliser before the roots can take it up.

Our products for this soil condition are Green Phosphate, the VAN NP Range, V-Phosphate and Fusion Phosphate.

87.8%

Potassium below 180 ppm

In the programme, 87.8% of the samples had less than 180 ppm of available potassium. Punjab's soil laboratories treat 180 ppm as the adequate level.

Our products for this soil condition are Vital Potash, Fusion Potash, SOP, VL-Potash Liquid and V-Potash Plus.

90.2%

Organic matter below 0.86%

In the programme, 90.2% of the samples had less than 0.86% organic matter. Punjab's soil laboratories rate organic matter below 0.86% as low. Organic matter holds nutrients and water in the soil.

Our products for this soil condition are Humi Grow, Humi Grow Plus, V-Compost and Tornado.

59.0%

Zinc below 1.0 ppm

In the programme's zinc results, 59.0% of the samples had less than 1.0 ppm of zinc. Under Punjab's working thresholds, zinc below 1.0 ppm is rated low. In Punjab's alkaline soils, zinc is held in chemical forms that roots cannot take up.

Our products for this soil condition are V-Transform, V-Zinc 10% and VL-Micromix.

51.7%

Boron below 0.5 ppm

In the programme, 51.7% of the samples had less than 0.5 ppm of boron. Under Punjab's working thresholds, boron below 0.5 ppm is rated low.

We supply boron as a coating on Vital Potash granules, as part of Fusion Potash, and on its own as VL-Boron.

Each share is a measurement from the Punjab soil testing programme. The explanation of what a soil condition does to a nutrient is our own. We base it on soil science.

02 · Soil chemistry

The chemistry of calcareous soil

We consider that, on Punjab's alkaline, calcareous soil, the form of a fertiliser and its placement affect how much nutrient reaches the crop more than the quantity applied does. The drawing below compares 2 forms of nitrogen fertiliser and 2 forms of phosphate fertiliser in soil above pH 8.

Drag the divider to compare the 2 sides. Choose a stage to move the drawing forward in time.

1 Dry soil

Plain urea and DAP

The left side of the drawing shows a granule of plain urea and a granule of diammonium phosphate (DAP) in dry soil.

Vital Urea and Green Phosphate

The right side shows a granule of Vital Urea and a granule of Green Phosphate in the same soil. The coating of Vital Urea does not react in dry soil.

2 The first irrigation

Plain urea and DAP

Plain urea dissolves in the first irrigation. All of its nitrogen is therefore exposed to loss at the same time. The soil around the DAP granule stays alkaline.

Vital Urea and Green Phosphate

Release of nitrogen from Vital Urea starts at the first irrigation or rain. The binder that holds the sulfur coating on the granule breaks down when water reaches it. The urea inside then starts to dissolve. The soil around the Green Phosphate granule is acidic at first.

3 After the first irrigation

Plain urea and DAP

In soil above pH 8, part of the nitrogen from plain urea is lost to the air as ammonia. The irrigation water carries another part down below the roots. Phosphate dissolving from a granule meets calcium within a few centimetres in soil above pH 8 that contains free lime. The calcium then binds the phosphate. Bound phosphate is no longer dissolved in the soil water.

Vital Urea and Green Phosphate

The nitrogen of Vital Urea is released over several days (VAN field observation). The fine elemental sulfur stays where the granule was placed and oxidises there. The acidic zone around the Green Phosphate granule is a few centimetres wide. The acidic zone slows the reaction of phosphate with calcium during the weeks a root needs to reach the granule.

Nitrogen: plain urea and Vital Urea

Volatilisation is the loss of nitrogen to the air as ammonia. Volatilisation acts only on nitrogen that is dissolved in the soil water. A granule of plain urea dissolves all at once.

Each Vital Urea granule is plain urea inside a coating of fine elemental sulfur, held on by a binder. The coating reduces how much nitrogen is exposed to volatilisation at any one time. The coating does not stop volatilisation. Once released into hot alkaline soil, the nitrogen from Vital Urea behaves like nitrogen from any other source.

The Vital Urea page

Phosphate: DAP and Green Phosphate

In calcareous soil, calcium binds phosphate. The bound phosphate slowly becomes unavailable to the crop. The first calcium phosphates that form are still fairly available to the crop.

Green Phosphate is granulated in an acidic mix and coated with humic material. The soil around each Green Phosphate granule is acidic at first. This acidic zone is limited to a few centimetres around the granule. The acidic zone does not change the result of a soil test.

Potassium: in production

We use recovered potassium as a regular raw material in our own manufacturing. Pakistan has no potash mine in production. We use the recovered potassium in place of imported potash.

We plan a new line for 2027. The new line is designed to recover 500 tonnes of potash a year from 5,000 tonnes of ash.

Silicon: research and development

Recovery of silicon from the same ash is still in research and development. The silicon recovery process has worked in our laboratory. We are preparing the process for the production line.

04 · Laboratory

LAB 336 and blind testing

Our laboratory is accredited by PNAC to ISO/IEC 17025:2017 under accreditation number LAB 336. The accreditation covers 8 of the laboratory's 21 fertiliser tests.

How to have a VAN product tested blind

To have one of our products tested blind, send 500 g of it to Sample Reception and book a normal paid test. Sample Reception works from a building separate from our laboratory. The sample is given a code in place of the product name. The analysts therefore do not know that the sample is our product.

  1. You send 500 g of the product to Sample Reception. The parcel has your name on it.

    The sample carries your name and the product name.

  2. The parcel is opened at Sample Reception. Sample Reception works from a separate building, at its own address. The address is 604 Al-Hafeez Heights, Gulberg III, Lahore.

    The sample carries your name and the product name.

  3. A laboratory code is generated for the sample. The code carries no information about the sender. Every brand marking stays at Sample Reception.

    The sample carries only a code.

  4. The sample is divided into 3 portions. 2 portions go to the laboratory for analysis. The third portion is kept untouched for 24 months. Each portion is labelled with the code only.

    The sample carries only a code.

  5. Each of the 2 analysts receives a portion and runs the test. Neither analyst sees the other's result until both results are submitted.

    The sample carries only a code.

  6. The 2 results are compared with each other. The Quality Control (QC) Manager then signs the report. The signed report shows only the code.

    The sample carries only a code.

  7. The code is linked back to your name when the report is released. Sample Reception forwards the report to you as a protected PDF.

    The report carries your name.
