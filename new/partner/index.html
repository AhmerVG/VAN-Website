How to have a VAN product tested blind

To have one of our products tested blind, send 500 g of it to Sample Reception and book a normal paid test. Sample Reception works from a building separate from our laboratory. The sample is given a code in place of the product name. The analysts therefore do not know that the sample is our product.

  1. The parcelYou send 500 g of the product to Sample Reception. The parcel has your name on it.
  2. Sample ReceptionThe parcel is opened at Sample Reception. Sample Reception works from a separate building, at its own address. The address is 604 Al-Hafeez Heights, Gulberg III, Lahore.
  3. The codeA laboratory code is generated for the sample. The code carries no information about the sender. Every brand marking stays at Sample Reception.
  4. Division into 3 portionsThe sample is divided into 3 portions. 2 portions go to the laboratory for analysis. The third portion is kept untouched for 24 months. Each portion is labelled with the code only.
  5. The 2 analystsEach of the 2 analysts receives a portion and runs the test. Neither analyst sees the other's result until both results are submitted.
  6. Comparison and signatureThe 2 results are compared with each other. The Quality Control (QC) Manager then signs the report. The signed report shows only the code.
  7. The reportThe code is linked back to your name when the report is released. Sample Reception forwards the report to you as a protected PDF.

03 · Coatings

Coatings we apply to the granule

The drawings are not to scale. The boron coatings are shown in teal to make them visible. The other colours follow the natural colours of the materials.

Sulfur-coated urea

We coat each urea granule with elemental sulfur. A starch-based binder holds the sulfur on the granule.

The granule starts to release nitrogen at the first irrigation or rain. In our field observations, the granule released its nitrogen over several days. Release is faster in warm, well-irrigated soil and slower in dry or cold soil.

The sulfur is also a nutrient for the crop.

  • Urea core
  • Elemental sulfur coating
  • Nitrogen leaving the granule

Boron-coated potash

We coat each potash granule with boron in 2 forms, sodium borate and calcium borate.

The inner coating is calcium borate. The outer coating is sodium borate.

Sodium borate releases boron quickly. Calcium borate releases boron slowly.

Because the boron is coated onto the granule, the boron and the potash do not separate in the bag.

  • Potash granule
  • Calcium borate coating
  • Sodium borate coating
  • Boron released quickly
  • Boron released slowly

Humic-coated phosphate

We granulate the phosphate in an acidic mix. We coat each granule with humic material.

The acidic mix makes the soil around each granule acidic at first. This acidic zone is a few centimetres wide.

A root needs some weeks to reach the granule. We formulate the granule so that the acidic zone slows the reaction of phosphate with the calcium in the soil during those weeks.

  • Phosphate granule
  • Humic coating
  • Acidic zone

Dual-coated potash

Each potash granule carries 2 boron coatings.

The inner coating is boric acid. The outer coating is sodium borate.

Boron-coated potash is a different product. Its inner coating is calcium borate.

Formulation K-50DC in our library is also a different product. It carries a boron coating and a silicon coating.

  • Potash granule
  • Boric acid coating
  • Sodium borate coating

N-26L and N-24L: liquid nitrogen for centre-pivot injection and foliar spray

N-26L and N-24L carry their nitrogen in 3 forms: nitrate, ammoniacal and urea. The 3 forms become available at different times, so a single injection supplies nitrogen over a period instead of all at once. This changes when the nitrogen becomes available, not how much is supplied. A fertiliser with a single form of nitrogen makes all of it available at once, which does not suit application under a centre pivot. Both grades carry boron in solution, and N-26L also carries zinc, so these micronutrients need no separate application.

P-44L: liquid foliar phosphorus, P₂O₅ 44% w/v (weight by volume)

In Pakistan, the main obstacle for phosphorus fertiliser is alkaline, calcareous soil, at roughly pH 7.9 to 8.4. In this soil, most of the phosphorus applied is fixed as calcium phosphates and never reaches the plant. Phosphorus applied to the leaf does not enter the soil, so the fixation reaction never starts.

PK-82: high P and K complex for the reproductive stage

Nutrition programmes in Pakistan are front-loaded: most of the nutrients are applied early in the season. Nitrogen and basal phosphorus are applied, and then the crop reaches the stage at which its potassium demand peaks: grain fill in wheat, boll development in cotton, tuber bulking in potato, and fruit set in mango and citrus. By that stage the programme supplies no further nutrients. Potassium is the nutrient most heavily depleted from Pakistan’s soils, and phosphorus is the most heavily fixed. At the reproductive stage, the crop’s supply of both nutrients therefore falls short at the same time.

PK-82 contains P₂O₅ 42% and K₂O 40%, a total of 82 nutrient units, which gives the code its number. It contains no nitrogen, so it can be applied at a stage when extra nitrogen would push vegetative growth at the wrong time. It is produced as a powder, a crystalline soluble and a liquid concentrate.

There is no trial figure for PK-82 yet. Its dose rate depends on the crop, region and application method, and is set for each programme.

2 NP grades that reduce phosphate fixation in different ways

Both grades address the same problem: most of the phosphorus applied to Pakistani soil is fixed as calcium phosphate before the crop can take it up. The first grade changes the timing of the phosphate supply; the second resists the fixation reaction.

NP-540 (NP 5:40): phosphate released at 2 rates

The phosphate is in 2 fractions that release at different rates. The first is available immediately, for the seedling root, which has to find phosphorus in its first weeks. The second releases slowly afterwards and is still supplying phosphorus when the crop’s demand peaks. A conventional basal phosphate releases its whole amount early, when fixation is fastest and the crop is smallest. This grade spreads the supply across the season from a single application. Its nitrogen content is low by design, because it is designed as a phosphorus product, not as a grade that balances nitrogen and phosphorus.

NP-838S (NP 8:38 with sulfur): phosphate formulated to resist fixation

Instead of changing the timing of the phosphate supply, this grade is formulated to resist the fixation reaction itself. Sulfur acidifies the soil immediately around the granule, where alkalinity would otherwise cause the phosphate to precipitate. The grade also contains our own agents that keep soil calcium away from the phosphate, so that the phosphate stays available to the plant for longer. Release is slow throughout, rather than split into 2 fractions. The sulfur is an active part of the formulation, and the crop also needs it as a nutrient.

There is no trial figure for either grade yet. The analyses shown are recorded in our internal product records, and the analysis report and specification sheet are available under an NDA. Both grades are made in granular, powder and crystalline form, in 50 kg and 1,000 kg packs.
