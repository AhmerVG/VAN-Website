01The 6 factors that shape a formulation

The 6 factors we consider first

A blend that suits cotton on a canal-irrigated sandy loam is wrong for cotton on saline land irrigated with tube-well water. We therefore start from these 6 factors.

FactorWhat it covers
CropThe nutrient ratio and form, matched to the crop species and to the variety’s nutrient demand through the season.
SoilThe soil’s pH, calcareousness, organic matter and texture, and the nutrient fixation they cause.
Growth stageGermination, early growth, grand growth, the reproductive stage or maturity: each stage needs a different product.
CoatingRelease control and deficiency correction applied as a coating on the granule, rather than blended in as separate granules.
StressThe level of stress the crop is expected to face, from moderate to severe. Heat during grain fill, for example, has to be allowed for in the formulation.
Water and deliveryCanal, tube well or both, and increasingly drip, centre pivot and drone. The delivery method determines the solubility and salt index the product needs.

04What we will not do

2 rules we do not change

A formulation developed for a client never reappears as a VAN brand or as a library code.

If a declared analysis will not hold for 12 months in storage in a Pakistani warehouse, we do not put it on the label. Instead, we tell you the analysis that will hold.

Start by telling us the problem: the crop, the stage, the soil and the price the market will pay. We work out the chemistry.
