Interactive charts of Pakistan’s nutrient data

The 12 interactive charts on this page cover fertiliser use, the balance between nutrients, crop yield per kg of fertiliser nutrient, nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) and soil potassium. In the national charts, you can choose the countries to compare with Pakistan. In every chart, you can change the year and open the numbers behind it. The charts give fertiliser use per acre and crop yields in maunds per acre.

The national picture · 01 Usage

How much fertiliser Pakistan uses

  • Fertiliser nutrient per acre of cropland. In 2023, Pakistan used 63.3 kg of fertiliser nutrient per acre of cropland, against a world average of 47.1 kg. Fertiliser nutrient means nitrogen (as N), phosphate (as P₂O₅) and potash (as K₂O), counted together. Of Pakistan’s 63.3 kg, 0.6 kg was potash.
  • Where Pakistan stands. In 2023, Pakistan used more nitrogen per acre than 93% of farming countries and more phosphate per acre than 88% of them. By contrast, Pakistan used more potash per acre than only 24% of farming countries. Farming countries are countries with at least 1 million hectares of cropland, or about 2.5 million acres. About 111 farming countries had data for 2023.

The national picture · 02 Nutrient balance

The balance between nutrients

  • Potash or phosphate per 100 kg of nitrogen. In 2023, Pakistan used 1.2 kg of potash for every 100 kg of nitrogen. The world average was 34.1 kg of potash for every 100 kg of nitrogen.
  • Nitrogen per 1 kg of phosphate. In 2023, Pakistan used 3.8 kg of nitrogen for every 1 kg of phosphate (P₂O₅). The balance that the National Fertilizer Development Centre (NFDC) recommends is 2 kg of nitrogen for every 1 kg of phosphate (NFDC Fertilizer Review 2024-25).

The national picture · 03 Productivity

Crop yield per kg of fertiliser nutrient

  • Cereal grain per kg of fertiliser nutrient. By a rough indicator, Pakistan got about 23 kg of cereal grain for each kg of fertiliser nutrient in 2023. The indicator is cereal yield per acre divided by the fertiliser nutrient used per acre of all cropland. On the same indicator, the world figure was 36 kg.
  • Fertiliser and yield. In the 2023 data of the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), 54 countries harvested more cereal per acre than Pakistan while using less fertiliser nutrient per acre.
  • Crop yields. Pakistan’s wheat yield in 2024 was 33.1 maunds per acre. The chart compares Pakistan’s wheat, rice, maize and sugarcane yields with those of any other country, for each year from 1961.
  • Grain per kg of nitrogen. By a rough indicator, Pakistan’s grain per kg of nitrogen was about 30 kg in 2023. The 30 kg is Pakistan’s cereal yield per acre divided by the nitrogen used per acre of all cropland, with both figures in kg per acre. The chart also compares yearly changes in nitrogen use with yearly changes in cereal yield. From 1962 to 1990, years with a larger rise in nitrogen use also tended to have a larger rise in cereal yield. From 1991 to 2023, the link between yearly changes in nitrogen use and yearly changes in cereal yield was weak.
  • Phosphate and yield. Across 107 farming countries in 2023, countries that used more phosphate per acre tended to harvest more cereal per acre. In VAN’s calculation from FAO data, the rank correlation between phosphate use per acre and cereal yield per acre was 0.60. On the rank correlation scale, 0 means no link and 1 means a perfect link. Pakistan used more phosphate per acre than most of the 107 farming countries.

The national picture · 04 Where nitrogen goes

Nitrogen use efficiency (NUE)

  • NUE. Pakistan’s NUE was 29.1% in 2023 (an FAO estimate). The 29.1% means that Pakistan’s harvested crops contained about 29 kg of nitrogen for every 100 kg of nitrogen added to cropland in 2023. The 100 kg counts nitrogen from all sources, including fertiliser and manure. The other 71 kg or so of every 100 kg added ended up outside the harvested crops. World NUE was 55.3% in 2023.

The state of the soil · 01 Potash removed and applied

Punjab’s potash balance, 1971-72 to 2023-24

  • Punjab potash balance. Punjab’s 5 main crops are wheat, rice, sugarcane, maize and cotton. In the 2023-24 crop year, these 5 crops removed an estimated 940,000 tonnes of potash (as K₂O) from the soil. In the same year, about 36,000 tonnes of potash was put back into the soil in fertiliser (NFDC offtake records). The chart shows model estimates for each year from 1971-72 to 2023-24.

Soil Poverty Series · Projection

Projected soil potassium in Punjab

  • Punjab soil potassium outlook. If potash fertiliser use in Punjab does not rise, the Soil Poverty report’s model projects that Punjab’s area-weighted average available soil potassium will fall below 100 parts per million (ppm). Available soil potassium is the potassium (K) extracted from soil with ammonium acetate, as Punjab soil laboratories report it, in milligrams per kilogram of soil (ppm). The report’s model made many separate calculations of the projection, called model runs. In each run, the model picked a value for every uncertain input from the range that the report gives for that input. In the model’s average case, the fall below 100 ppm comes around the crop year 2035-36. The average case is the median of the model runs. In any year, half of the model runs project a lower level of available soil potassium than the average case. In the worst case, the fall comes in 2026-27. In the best case, the fall comes in 2043-44. In any year, 1 in 10 model runs projects a lower level of available soil potassium than the worst case. Another 1 in 10 runs projects a higher level than the best case. These years are model projections. On VAN’s 5-band soil-test scale, 100 ppm is the boundary between VAN band 3 of 5 (Average) and VAN band 2 of 5 (Weak).
